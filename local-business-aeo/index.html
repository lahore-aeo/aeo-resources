AEO basics for local businesses

Local discoverability begins with consistent, useful business information. Clear service pages and an accurate business profile help customers decide whether to contact you.

Keep business facts consistent

Use the same real business name, address or service area, phone number, hours, and service descriptions across the website and eligible business listings. Correct outdated details rather than adding location pages that imply offices or services the business does not have.

Build useful service and location pages

Each page should explain the service, who it helps, what is included, relevant limits, and how to get started. A location page needs specific local usefulness—such as service coverage, local process details, or genuine examples—not just a city name swapped into boilerplate.

Make answers easy to find

Answer common pre-contact questions directly: what areas are served, when the business is open, how pricing is scoped, what a first appointment includes, and how a customer can reach the business. Link from each service page to relevant local information and back to the main service overview.

For a Google Business Profile, follow Google's eligibility and representation rules. Do not create fake locations, reviews, or service claims to widen coverage.

Measure the real customer journey

Track calls, forms, directions, qualified visits, and search queries where reporting is available. Review business facts when hours, service areas, or contact details change. For the larger strategy, see topic cluster planning and visibility measurement.
