Metrics and reporting

Search performance metrics: what to track and report

A useful report explains what changed, which pages or queries changed, and whether the change mattered to visitors or the business.

Track metrics by purpose

  • Search reach: impressions and the queries and pages receiving them.
  • Search visits: clicks, click-through rate, and average position as directional Search Console metrics.
  • Visitor quality: engaged or qualified visits and meaningful actions, using a consistent analytics definition.
  • Business outcomes: leads, sales, or another conversion that matters to the site.
  • AI answer checks: a dated sample of fixed questions, platforms, cited URLs, and citation accuracy. Treat this as a sample, not a complete visibility share.

Use a consistent reporting view

Compare equivalent time windows and annotate site releases, campaigns, seasonality, and tracking changes. Break results down by landing page and query when possible. Avoid combining unlike metrics into a made-up score; impressions, citations, and conversions describe different outcomes.

Turn the report into an action

  1. Identify the clearest change and the page or query behind it.
  2. Check whether the change is large enough and sustained enough to investigate.
  3. Look for plausible causes, including technical changes and demand shifts.
  4. Choose one evidence-based update, record its date, and review the same measures later.

A report is useful when it helps you decide what to improve. A higher visibility number alone is not proof that content changes caused better outcomes.

Simple monthly report template

For each priority page, record: reporting dates; target questions; impressions; clicks; qualified visits; conversions; sampled AI citations and accuracy; notable changes; next action; review date. Leave fields blank when reliable data is unavailable instead of estimating.
